Singular value decomposition

Every matrix is a rotation, a stretch, and another rotation.

Hand any matrix a sphere and it gives you back an ellipsoid. The SVD says exactly how: turn the sphere so the right directions face the axes, stretch along each axis, then turn again. Nothing else ever happens. Drag the scenes to orbit them.

Matrix A (edit me)

Try

1 · Vᵀ rotates

A rigid turn. Lengths and angles survive. It brings each vᵢ onto a coordinate axis, so the next step can stretch along clean, perpendicular directions.

2 · Σ stretches

A diagonal matrix: scale axis i by σᵢ. This is the only step that changes size. A zero σ crushes a whole dimension; that is what rank deficiency looks like.

3 · U rotates

Another rigid turn, carrying the stretched axes to their final directions uᵢ. So A vᵢ = σᵢ uᵢ: each input arrow lands on an output arrow, scaled.

SVD finds the shape of a cloud

Stack 800 points as rows of a matrix X (centered). The right singular vectors of X are the cloud's own axes, longest first, and σᵢ/√(n−1) is the spread along each. This is PCA. Dropping the small σ's flattens the cloud onto the plane or line that loses the least.

The statement

Any real m×n matrix factors as A = UΣVᵀ, with U and V orthogonal (rotations, possibly with a mirror) and Σ diagonal with σ₁ ≥ σ₂ ≥ … ≥ 0. Equivalently A = Σᵢ σᵢ uᵢ vᵢᵀ. Truncating that sum after k terms gives the best rank-k approximation in both the Frobenius and spectral norms (Eckart–Young), which is why the same factorization powers image compression, PCA, recommender systems, least squares and LoRA.

Everything on this page is computed live in your browser with one-sided Jacobi, no precomputed data. In scene 1 the factors are adjusted so both turns are proper rotations: when det A < 0 the mirror is moved into Σ as a negative σ₃, which you see as the shape passing through flat.

The original guided tour, with movie recommendations, eigenvectors and Fourier series, is still at /svd/classic.
